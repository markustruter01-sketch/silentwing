[P-31] Stage A3 — real glass, matching the reference.

GOAL
The current panels are dark opaque boxes. The target is genuine
glassmorphism: you can see the background through the panel, softly
blurred, with a light tint and a thin bright edge. Much lighter than what
is there now.

RISK CLASS
[STRUCTURAL-ONLY] — visual only.

DONE WHEN
The background is visibly readable through every HTML panel, text stays
legible, and the frame rate has not dropped more than 10%.

SCOPE
- panel and label styling only

DO NOT
- Do not change aircraft positions, altitude, scale, or any logic.
- Do not change label positions. Overlapping labels are the NEXT prompt,
  not this one.
- Do not change how the app looks with photoreal ground OFF.
- Do not apply backdrop-filter to anything per-aircraft or per-frame.
- Do not sacrifice legibility for transparency. If text becomes hard to
  read over bright imagery, pull the transparency back and say so.
- Do not create or commit any documentation, notes or summary file.

STEPS
1. Report the frame rate with photoreal ground ON, averaged over ten
   seconds, before any change.
2. Report the current fill opacity used on the HTML panels. The present
   look is too opaque — the background cannot be seen through it at all.

STEPS — the glass itself
3. Restyle the fixed HTML panels — METAR stack, sequence ladder, alert
   panels, keyboard help, photoreal control — to this specification:
   - background fill at 25-35% opacity, NOT 60%. The background must be
     visibly present through the panel.
   - tint the fill toward the existing dark blue-grey rather than black,
     so it reads as tinted glass and not as a shadow
   - backdrop-filter: blur(24px) saturate(150%) — a larger blur than
     before, which is what allows lower opacity to stay readable
   - a 1px border, white at 15-18% opacity
   - the top edge brighter than the rest: add a subtle light gradient
     across the top few pixels of each panel, which is what sells the
     "lit glass" look in the reference
   - a soft outer shadow, large and low opacity, to lift the panel
   - keep the existing corner radius
4. Text legibility at that transparency needs help. Add a subtle dark
   text-shadow to body text inside the panels. Keep it subtle — it should
   be invisible as an effect and only do its job.
5. Where a panel contains a dense data table, the fill may sit slightly
   more opaque than a panel of headings. Use judgement, report what you
   chose for each.

STEPS — the floating 3D labels
6. These are canvas textures and cannot blur. Lighten them to match the
   new direction rather than leaving them as heavy dark boxes:
   - reduce the panel opacity from its current value to roughly 55-65%
   - keep the 1px light border
   - keep the text shadow, which is doing the legibility work
   Report the before and after values.
7. Confirm this is drawn at texture creation, not per frame.

STEPS — verify
8. Measure the frame rate again, same method. Report both numbers. If the
   drop exceeds 10%, reduce the blur radius until it does not, and report
   what you settled on.
9. Screenshot with photoreal ground ON at Sydney, from an angle where a
   panel sits over bright imagery — that is where glass either works or
   does not.
10. Screenshot with photoreal ground OFF, confirming no change.
11. Run the tests, typecheck and build as usual.
12. Commit. Message: "Stage A3: genuine glassmorphic panels
    [STRUCTURAL-ONLY]". Do not push.

REPORT

- Frame rate before and after.
- Opacity values before and after, for both HTML panels and 3D labels.
- Both screenshots.
- Whether legibility held at the target transparency, honestly. If you had

- Raw output of step 11.
